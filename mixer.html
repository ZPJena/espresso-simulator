Flow Pattern Explains Performance

In a symmetric rotational flow (no baffle), fluid elements follow closed circular trajectories. Mixing is limited by molecular diffusion across shear layers, which is extremely slow.

Adding a baffle breaks the circular symmetry, deflecting the streamlines. The combination of rotation and deflection creates chaotic advection, stretching the milk droplets into thin sheets (striations) and folding them.

Because the striation thickness decreases exponentially, the diffusion time drops quadratically: \[t_{\text{diff}} \approx \frac{s^2}{D}\] This explains why flow design determines the rate of thermal or chemical homogenization.
